Leonardo Oliveira

X-ray diffraction and imaging scientist
Postdoctoral researcher at Lund University and MAX IV Laboratory, NanoMAX beamline

MAX IV Laboratory

Lund, Sweden

I am an X-ray diffraction and imaging scientist with 7+ years of laboratory and synchrotron experience linking structure, processing and properties in advanced materials.

My core methods are Rietveld and Le Bail refinement, texture analysis and in situ diffraction, together with strain and tilt mapping by dark-field X-ray microscopy (DFXM) and scanning nano-diffraction (nano-XRD). As a postdoc at the NanoMAX beamline at MAX IV, I run in situ experiments with users, assess their feasibility, and develop Python tools for processing data during beamtime.

My research focuses on how local structure, strain and residual stress govern functionality in ferroelectrics, antiferroelectrics and other electromechanical materials, from lead-free dielectric ceramics for energy storage to Hf0.5Zr0.5O2 device stacks and InGaN nanoLEDs.

I did my PhD in Physics at the Technical University of Denmark (DTU), where I quantified heterogeneity in lead-free dielectric ceramics for energy storage using synchrotron X-rays.
